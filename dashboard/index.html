What the driver sees

concept
No telltale

Security detail is not shown to the driver: at most a generic service telltale while an event is open. CAN IDs, attack type and evidence go to the V-SOC (UN R155 / ISO 21434; no distraction, ISO 26262). A design concept; no vehicle runs this.

What this is. A replay of recorded CAN traffic from the public HCRL Car-Hacking dataset (one vehicle, recorded in 2016) with the real output of GuardCAN's detectors. A proof of concept evaluated offline; it is not connected to a vehicle and nothing here ran in one.

Where every number comes from. dashboard/export_replay.py loads the saved time-split models, runs both detectors on the test part (the last 30 % of every recording, never used for fitting), checks that it reproduces the windowed events and per-frame false alarms in bench/hcrl/windowed/metrics.json, and exports per-ID frame counts per 100 ms window, the learned ceilings, the ground-truth injection intervals, the windowed events and the per-frame alarms for five ~60–90 s stretches. The headline tiles are the whole test part from that metrics.json. Nothing on this page is generated or random. Details: dashboard/README.md, docs/windowed-detection.md, the repository.

Data and citation. Traffic: the HCRL Car-Hacking Dataset, Hacking and Countermeasure Research Lab (HCRL), Korea University, recorded from a real vehicle through its OBD-II port. Source paper: H. M. Song, J. Woo and H. K. Kim, “In-vehicle network intrusion detection using deep convolutional neural network,” Vehicular Communications, vol. 21, 100198, 2020, doi:10.1016/j.vehcom.2019.100198. Only aggregated counts derived from the dataset are published here (frames per CAN ID per 100 ms window, and the times and counts of injection intervals, events and alarms, with the CAN IDs and log times they refer to); no CAN frames or payloads, and the dataset itself is not redistributed. Download it from HCRL under its own terms.

Limits. One vehicle and one attack style (high-rate injection). The windowed detector cannot see an attacker who silences the real ECU and sends at the normal rate, does not check for IDs going silent, and stops detecting injection below about 10 extra frames/s on a 100 frames/s ID (derived test). Zero false events in HCRL's 0.4 h of attack-free test traffic bound the rate only to < 7.6 per hour; the ≤ 1 per vehicle-hour bar needs ≥ 3 h of attack-free data to show.
